### **Distributed Tracing with Jaeger**

24. **Q:** Your microservices are deployed in Kubernetes, and you're observing inconsistent traces in Jaeger, where some services have missing spans. What could be the cause, and how would you resolve it?  
   **A:** The issue could be caused by missing trace context propagation or incomplete service instrumentation. Ensure that all services are correctly instrumented with Jaeger and that the trace context is passed with each request.

25. **Q:** You’re seeing increased latency in a specific microservice, but logs alone do not provide enough context. How can Jaeger help you identify the source of the problem?  
   **A:** By analyzing the distributed traces in Jaeger, you can trace the request flow across services and identify the specific service or operation that is introducing latency, providing more context than logs alone.

26. **Q:** You want to track a user’s journey through multiple services, and you need to ensure that the trace data is collected and linked across service boundaries. How would you achieve this with Jaeger?  
   **A:** Ensure that all services are instrumented to propagate the trace context (trace ID, span ID) with each request. This will allow Jaeger to correlate spans across services, providing a full trace of the user journey.

27. **Q:** A performance degradation issue is reported, and you need to see where requests are getting delayed. How do you configure Jaeger to give you the most accurate information?  
   **A:** You can enable **low-latency sampling** in Jaeger to capture detailed trace information without excessive overhead. Additionally, ensure that all critical services are instrumented and trace data is captured for every request.

28. **Q:** You are troubleshooting a microservices application and you see "trace missing" errors in Jaeger. What might be the cause, and how would you address it?  
   **A:** Missing traces may be due to incorrect trace propagation, network issues, or missing instrumentation. Verify that trace IDs are properly passed between services, and ensure that all services are correctly instrumented with Jaeger.

### **ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana)**

29. **Q:** Your team is ingesting logs from multiple microservices using Logstash, but some logs are not appearing in Kibana. How do you troubleshoot this issue?  
   **A:** Check the Logstash configuration for any parsing or filtering errors. Ensure that Logstash is successfully sending logs to Elasticsearch and verify that the correct index pattern is set up in Kibana. Review the Logstash logs for any errors.

30. **Q:** The volume of logs being generated by your application has significantly increased, leading to slower Elasticsearch queries in Kibana. How would you optimize the performance of your ELK Stack?  
   **A:** Implement **index lifecycle management (ILM)** to manage log retention, use **log filtering** in Logstash to reduce unnecessary log data, and optimize Elasticsearch indices by adjusting shard settings and using **query caching**.

31. **Q:** You want to visualize logs from different services in a unified view in Kibana. How would you correlate logs from various microservices to provide a complete view of a request’s lifecycle?  
   **A:** Use **trace IDs** and **span IDs** (from Jaeger) in your logs to correlate them across services. Ensure that these IDs are included in the logs by using a custom log format, and then use Kibana's filtering capabilities to correlate logs based on these IDs.

32. **Q:** You notice that some logs in Kibana are being ingested with incorrect timestamps, making it difficult to correlate events in your system. How would you resolve this issue?  
   **A:** Check the Logstash timestamp parsing filters to ensure that timestamps are correctly parsed. Verify that the **timezone** and **timestamp format** are configured properly in Logstash. Also, check if there are issues with the log source systems' clock or time zone configuration.

33. **Q:** In your ELK Stack, you notice that Elasticsearch is receiving a large number of log entries with high cardinality, such as user IDs. How would you manage this data efficiently to prevent performance degradation?  
   **A:** You can use **field exclusion** or **field anonymization** in Logstash to reduce high cardinality data. Additionally, use **index templates** in Elasticsearch to limit the number of unique fields indexed and prevent unnecessary performance overhead.

### **Advanced Scenario-Based Questions**

34. **Q:** Your team is facing sporadic system failures, but logs show no clear error message. How can you use Jaeger and ELK Stack together to investigate the root cause of the failures?  
   **A:** Use Jaeger to trace the request flow and identify where failures are occurring in the distributed system. Then, correlate the traces with logs in the ELK Stack to get additional context about the service’s state and any exceptions or warnings that might help pinpoint the issue.

35. **Q:** Your application’s response times have been increasing, and you are unsure if it’s due to network issues, slow database queries, or service inefficiencies. How would you leverage Jaeger and the ELK Stack to troubleshoot this?  
   **A:** Use Jaeger for distributed tracing to measure latency at each service level and pinpoint where delays occur. Then, use Kibana to analyze logs for signs of issues, such as long database query times or network failures, to correlate with the slow responses.

36. **Q:** You’ve noticed a discrepancy between log data in Kibana and trace data in Jaeger. While Jaeger shows long request times, Kibana doesn’t indicate any errors. What might be happening?  
   **A:** This discrepancy could be due to missed or incomplete logs in Kibana, or the log data might not be correlated correctly with the trace data in Jaeger. Verify that trace IDs and span IDs are properly included in your logs and are being propagated correctly.

37. **Q:** Your system is experiencing a sudden spike in traffic, and you're seeing performance degradation in your microservices. How would you use Jaeger and Kibana to diagnose and resolve the issue?  
   **A:** Start by using Jaeger to identify latency bottlenecks and trace the requests causing delays. In Kibana, examine the logs for any errors, high resource consumption, or service crashes that might be related to the spike in traffic. Correlate both sources to identify the root cause.

38. **Q:** Your team has implemented Jaeger for distributed tracing in a microservices architecture, but you're not getting detailed tracing for certain services. How would you go about troubleshooting the issue?  
   **A:** First, ensure that all microservices are properly instrumented with Jaeger and that the trace context is propagated correctly across service boundaries. Check if the Jaeger agents/collectors are properly configured and whether there are network or sampling issues that could be affecting trace collection.

39. **Q:** You are dealing with multiple logs coming from different environments (development, staging, and production), and you need to monitor them separately in Kibana. How would you set this up?  
   **A:** You can use **log tags** or **environment variables** in your log configuration to distinguish logs from different environments. In Logstash, apply filters to tag logs based on the environment, and create separate index patterns in Kibana for each environment.

40. **Q:** You are trying to investigate an incident that only occurred in a specific time window, and the logs in Kibana are overwhelming. How would you narrow down your search to focus on the relevant data?  
   **A:** Use Kibana’s **time filter** to restrict the logs to the relevant time window. Additionally, you can filter logs based on severity or service name, or use **tags** like trace or span IDs (from Jaeger) to correlate logs with specific requests or incidents.

41. **Q:** Your team uses Jaeger and ELK Stack, but you're noticing delays in processing trace data and logs, leading to slower investigation times. What could be causing this, and how would you optimize performance?  
   **A:** Performance issues could be due to heavy trace and log ingestion or inefficient storage/query configurations. Optimize Elasticsearch indices by tuning settings (shards, replicas, etc.), reduce unnecessary logging in Logstash, and consider enabling trace sampling in Jaeger to reduce the data volume.

42. **Q:** A user reports that they experience slow page loads, and you suspect that a microservice might be the root cause. How would you use Jaeger and the ELK Stack together to investigate and identify the problem?  
   **A:** Use Jaeger to trace the slow requests and pinpoint which microservice is responsible for the delay. In Kibana, look for logs from the affected service during the time window when the issue occurred, and search for any resource utilization anomalies, errors, or warnings that correlate with the slow response times.

### **Optimization and Scalability**

43. **Q:** Your system’s load has increased significantly, and Elasticsearch is starting to lag when querying logs in Kibana. How would you scale the ELK Stack to handle the increased load?  
   **A:** You would scale Elasticsearch by adding more nodes to the cluster, optimize shard allocation, and use index lifecycle management (ILM) to efficiently manage data retention. Additionally, ensure Kibana is optimized for large datasets by fine-tuning query performance and enabling caching where appropriate.

44. **Q:** The Jaeger traces for a specific service in your microservices environment are being delayed. How would you address this performance issue to ensure timely trace collection?  
   **A:** Investigate network latency between the service and Jaeger collectors, ensure that the service is properly instrumented, and check if trace sampling rates need to be adjusted to reduce overhead. Also, ensure Jaeger’s storage backend is performant and properly scaled.
